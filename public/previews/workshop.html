Chapter 2: The Four Movements
The workshop is organized around four movements:
1.
Knowledge
2.
Understanding
3.
Wisdom
4.
Evaluation
These movements describe what the thinking is trying to accomplish.
They are not rigid compartments. You may move forward, return to something you
missed and then continue again.
Their purpose is to help you recognize where you are and what kind of work may be
needed next.
Knowledge
Knowledge gathers material for the work.
It may come from:
•
questions;
•
observation;
•
interviews;
•
customer experiences;
•
business records;
•
research;
•
measurement;
•
experiments;
•
professional advice;
•
brainstorming; or
•
ideas that deserve further examination.
Knowledge includes more than established facts.
An observation may be useful without proving a conclusion. An idea may be worth
considering without being supported yet. An assumption may need to be identified
before it can be tested. An unknown may show you what to investigate next.

Chapter 3: Finding the Right Tool
You do not need to learn every method before using the workshop.
You only need to identify the work in front of you and know where to look.
Begin with the situation
State what requires attention.
Keep it plain.
For example:
•
Sales have declined for three months.
•
Customers are leaving during onboarding.
•
I am considering adding a new service.
•
The business has more work than it can reliably complete.
•
A plan produced a different result than I expected.
•
I have several opportunities and cannot pursue all of them.
You do not need to explain the entire situation before beginning. State enough to
make the next question visible.
Ask what the thinking needs to do
Use the four movements as the first sorting decision.
Choose Knowledge when you need to:
•
gather information;
•
ask better questions;
•
hear from customers or employees;
•
examine records;
•
observe what is happening;
•
generate ideas;
•
identify unknowns; or
•
test an assumption.
